# SettleHex brand context

## Product

SettleHex is a free online Catan game currently focused on quick 1v1 play. The
duel boards are deliberately balanced. Players can play a friend, find an
online opponent, or challenge the Puffer bot. Four-player and larger games are
expected later, but the current beta should be represented honestly.

Current primary descriptor:

> Free online Catan for quick 1v1 games.

Current supporting proof:

> Balanced boards. Play a friend, find a match, or challenge Puffer.

## Desired personality

The identity should feel like a friendly modern strategy game:

- joyful and warm;
- polished and confident;
- clearly connected to a colourful tabletop board;
- playful without becoming childish, bubbly, or toy-like;
- modern without becoming a sharp, generic SaaS or esports identity.

The real game board and in-match interface are currently stronger brand
references than the homepage logo treatment.

## Current typography

- Outfit is the global product and interface font.
- Fredoka was deliberately introduced only for the homepage `Settlehex`
  wordmark and the `Sx` glyph because Outfit initially felt too sharp there.
- The social card uses a large Outfit `SettleHex` wordmark and currently looks
  good.
- Human-readable product naming should now consistently use `SettleHex`, with a
  capital H.

Fredoka is not automatically rejected. The question is whether it remains the
best display wordmark face once the symbol and lockup are redesigned.

## Current symbol problem

The homepage currently pairs the Fredoka wordmark with a glossy green rounded
hex containing a white `Sx`. The favicon uses the same construction.

Problems observed:

- the Fredoka plus glossy `Sx` badge combination feels more like a children's
  mobile app than a friendly modern strategy client;
- `Sx` becomes an indistinct white shape at tiny favicon size;
- the browser-tab mark appears optically small;
- the mark, wordmark, favicon, and Outfit-only social card do not yet feel like
  one intentional responsive identity system.

The goal is not to force an identical composition onto every surface. A
wordmark, standalone symbol, homepage lockup, favicon, and social card may use
responsive variants as long as they clearly belong to the same system.

## Current exploration

The most promising idea is a negative-space `S`:

1. Begin with a field or compact arrangement derived from rounded board
   hexagons.
2. Crop it to a compact outer silhouette, probably a soft square/squircle for
   favicon use or a rounded hex for a more board-native version.
3. Subtract a proper typographic `S` as an empty channel through the field.
4. Allow the crop to leave complete hexagons, half hexagons, corners, and
   hexagon-shaped fragments. They do not all need to remain recognisable full
   tiles.

The `S` should use a considered real glyph outline—initially compare Fredoka and
Outfit—rather than an improvised river-like Bézier curve.

This construction might create a distinctive SettleHex mark while avoiding the
common "coloured hex with a white initial" pattern. It remains a hypothesis,
not an approved answer.

An alternate idea worth retaining is a soft square/squircle containing a simple
hexagonal aperture. It is clear at favicon size but may be too generic unless
the geometry becomes more ownable.

## Competitive distinction

Colonist's favicon is a flat blue regular hex with a centred white `C`.
SettleHex should not merely reproduce that construction with green, rounded
corners, and an `S`. Corner rounding alone is not enough differentiation at
16px.

## Surface requirements

The system must be judged in context:

- homepage lockup at roughly 48–64px symbol size;
- standalone mark at 64px and 32px;
- actual favicon at 16px and 32px, including optical fill and transparent
  padding;
- `SettleHex` wordmark beside the mark;
- Outfit-only or system-consistent social-card wordmark;
- light blue/sky homepage background and light neutral share-card panel.

Large isolated logo renders are insufficient evidence.
